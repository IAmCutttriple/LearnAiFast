THE BASICS, WITHOUT THE BUZZWORDS

So… what is AI?

Think of AI as a pattern-finding helper. It learns from examples, then uses what it learned to help with a new task.

◉01 / THE IDEA

A clever pattern finder.

Artificial intelligence is technology that spots patterns in information and uses them to answer questions, make predictions, or create something new. It’s not magic—and it can still get things wrong.

EXAMPLES→A useful pattern
↗02 / WHY IT HELPS

More time for the good stuff.

  • Turns repetitive work into a quick first draft.
  • Gets your creativity unstuck with fresh angles.
  • Offers patient, on-demand explanations—like a 24/7 study buddy.
⌁03 / EVERYDAY AI

Already part of your day.

  • Summarize a long set of class notes.
  • Build a realistic study schedule.
  • Draft an email or practice a new language.
  • Brainstorm project ideas when you’re stuck.
⟡04 / MAKE IT EASIER

Big task? Smaller steps.

Let AI take the first pass at routine work, or ask it to break a tricky topic into bite-sized explanations. You stay in charge; it helps you find your next step.

Complex task→Small steps

Knowing how to ask, check, and use AI thoughtfully can help you at school and at work. The advantage isn’t using AI for everything—it’s knowing when it helps, where it falls short, and how to make the result your own.

THE PROMPTING PLAYBOOK

Say it better.
Get there faster.

A prompt is simply the instruction you give an AI. A little more detail can turn a generic answer into something genuinely useful.

FIVE BUILDING BLOCKS

A great prompt has a plan.

You don’t need all five every time. Add the pieces that make your goal clear.

01♙

Role / Persona

Who should the AI be?

“Act as a patient tutor…”
02◎

Clear task

What should it do?

“Explain photosynthesis…”
03▤

Context

What should it know?

“I’m in year 9 biology…”
04⊞

Constraints

What are the guardrails?

“Keep it under 200 words…”
05▤

Output format

How should it look?

“Use 5 bullet points…”
A QUICK BEFORE & AFTER

Same idea. Better direction.

THE VAGUE ASK WEAK
“Write about space.”

Hard to know what the writer needs, who it’s for, or where to begin.

THE THOUGHTFUL ASK STRONGER
“Act as an encouraging science teacher. Write a 250-word introduction to black holes for a curious 13-year-old who knows the basics of gravity. Use one everyday analogy and finish with three surprising facts. Format it in short paragraphs with a clear heading.”

It gives the AI a role, audience, scope, detail, and a clear shape for the answer.
